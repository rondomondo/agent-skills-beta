Paper Plane Facts

Paper planes (also called paper aircraft or paper darts) are folded paper gliders. Here are key facts:

WORLD RECORDS
- The world record for longest flight time is 29.2 seconds, set by Takuo Toda in 2009
- The world distance record is 226 feet 10 inches (69.14 metres), set by Joe Ayoob in 2012 using a design by John Collins ("The Paper Airplane Guy")
- The altitude record: paper planes have been launched from near-space (30km) via weather balloon

DESIGN & AERODYNAMICS
- Paper planes work using the same four forces as real aircraft: lift, drag, thrust, and gravity
- The Nakamura Lock is a classic folding technique that locks the nose of the plane
- The dart design is the fastest, while gliders optimize for hang time
- Dihedral angle (wings angled upward) improves stability
- Centre of gravity must be forward of the centre of lift for stable flight
- Camber (curved wing surface) increases lift

TYPES OF PAPER PLANES
- Dart: fast and streamlined
- Glider: wide wings, slow and floaty
- Stunt plane: designed for loops and tricks
- Hammerhead: blunt-nosed for stability
- Interceptor: sharp nose for speed
- Stealth: wide delta-wing shape

FAMOUS DESIGNS
- Nakamura Lock: Japanese classic, very reliable
- Suzanne: John Collins design, used in world records
- White Dove: gentle glider, great for beginners
- F-15 Eagle: realistic military replica fold
- The Harrier: acrobatic design, can loop and roll
- Concorde: swept-back delta wing inspired by the real aircraft

PAPER TYPES
- Standard 80gsm A4 is the most common
- Heavier paper (100gsm) adds momentum but reduces glide
- Lighter paper may crumple on impact
- Origami paper is thinner and holds creases more sharply
- Foil-backed paper adds weight and shine

HISTORY
- Paper planes are believed to date back to ancient China around 2000 years ago
- Leonardo da Vinci is said to have made paper models to test aircraft designs
- The modern paper plane hobby gained popularity post-WWII
- NASA has used paper plane aerodynamics in research
- Japan's Takuo Toda founded the Japan Origami Airplane Association

PHYSICS TERMS
- Lift: upward force generated by wing shape
- Drag: air resistance slowing the plane
- Thrust: the force you give the plane when you throw it
- Stall: when the plane's angle of attack is too steep and it loses lift
- Bernoulli principle: faster airflow over the wing creates lower pressure = lift
- Reynolds number: measures airflow turbulence at small scales

COMPETITIONS
- Red Bull Paper Wings: global paper plane throwing competition
- Japan Origami Airplane Association holds annual contests
- Categories include: distance, flight time, and aerobatics
- Paper plane throwing technique matters: spin vs flat release
- Annual World Paper Airplane Championships held in Seattle

THROWING TECHNIQUES
- Overhand: fast throw for distance
- Flat release: smooth and level for time aloft
- Spin release: adds rotation for stability
- Nose-down launch: for looping stunt designs
- Angle of release: 30–45 degrees optimal for distance
